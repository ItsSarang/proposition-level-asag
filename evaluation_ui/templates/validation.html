Double-blind human annotation standard for validating proposition classification accuracy.

1. Study Design & Protocol Architecture

The Mohler CS Short Answer Dataset v2.0 provides holistic answer-level grades (0–5) from human graders, but contains no proposition-level ground truth. To formally test whether the proposition-level classifier accurately categorizes unmatched information, we designed a 50-proposition double-blind human annotation protocol across 10 diverse computer science questions.

Step 1 Stratified Sampling

50 clean unmatched student propositions sampled across 10 diverse questions.

→
Step 2 Strict Double-Blinding

annotator_1.csv & annotator_2.csv generated with ZERO model predictions, rationales, or scores.

→
Step 3 Independent Human Annotation

Two human annotators label each statement into 4 categories using strict guidelines.

→
Step 4 Inter-Annotator & Classifier Evaluation

Computes Cohen's κ agreement, followed by Precision, Recall, and F1 against gold labels.

2. Four-Category Annotation Standard

Category Annotation Definition Epistemic Boundary
CORRECT_ADDITIONAL Factually valid domain assertion relevant to prompt, but unmentioned in the reference answer. Absent from reference ≠ Incorrect. Unsupported ≠ Automatically false.
IRRELEVANT Peripheral, historical, or off-topic information providing no academic value toward the learning objective. May be factually true in isolation, but off-topic for the specific question.
INCORRECT Direct domain falsehood, conceptual error, or assertion that contradicts the reference answer or CS principles. Requires positive evidence of error; never assign solely due to absence from reference.
UNCERTAIN Grammatically fragmented, ambiguous, or deictic statement where student intent cannot be verified fairly. Annotator must not guess intent when syntactic evidence is insufficient.
